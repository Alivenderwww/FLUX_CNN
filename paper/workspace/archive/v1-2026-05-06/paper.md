## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Motivation: PE Underutilization in Fixed Arrays + Streaming Constraints on Edge FPGAs

Fixed two-dimensional MAC arrays — typically organized as a 16×16 INT8 grid on mid-range edge FPGAs — have become the dominant building block for embedded CNN inference accelerators, including open-source designs such as Gemmini [Gemmini-DAC21] and VTA [VTA-MICRO19], as well as industrial reference architectures such as NVDLA [NVDLA]. Their popularity follows from two pragmatic properties: a square INT8 systolic grid maps cleanly onto FPGA DSP slices, and a fixed shape simplifies place-and-route closure. However, the achievable throughput of such an accelerator on real workloads is jointly bounded by two constraints that are not captured by peak-GOPS numbers — the spatial PE utilization actually attained per layer, and the on-chip SRAM available to stage feature maps. These two constraints, rather than peak compute, motivate the design of FLUX_CNN.

The first constraint is that, on ResNet-18-style networks, the spatial PE utilization of a fixed 16×16 array drops to between 12.5% and 50% in the shallow layers, where the input channel count Cin is far smaller than the array width. This pathology is the central motivation for narrative A of this paper: hardware that is sized for the bulk of a network spends a substantial fraction of its lifetime running at one-eighth to one-half of its nominal throughput, and the layers in question are precisely those whose spatial dimensions (H×W) are largest, so their cycle cost dominates end-to-end latency.

A quantitative breakdown on a representative ResNet-18-style network makes the pathology explicit. Layer 1 (K=7, Cin=4, Cout=8) attains only 32 of 256 useful MACs per cycle, i.e., 12.5% PE utilization; Layers 3 and 4 (Cin=8) reach 25%; the early stages of Layer 5 (Cin=8, Cout=16) reach 50%; only from Layer 5b onward, when Cin saturates the array width at 16, does utilization reach 100% <!-- 来自 contributions.md §4.4 / model_analysis.md §2 -->. Compiler-side folding can in principle push the shallow-layer numbers close to 100%, but the precise post-fold percentages depend on cycle counts that have not yet been re-extracted from regression runs [CHECK: Layer 1 / 3 / 4 / 5a / 5c 启用 Ky-fold 后实测 PE 利用率百分比，contributions.md §8.1 #1, #2].

The second constraint is on-chip memory. A mid-range edge FPGA such as the Xilinx XC7K325T provides roughly 445 BRAM36 tiles, equivalent to about 1.6 MB of SRAM, of which a single FLUX_CNN core already consumes 128 BRAM36 (28.8%) for weight, input, shortcut, and partial-sum buffers <!-- 来自 contributions.md §4.1 / STATUS.md line 32-44 -->. A single VGA-resolution 480×640 INT8 input image at 16 channels occupies roughly 4.9 MB, comfortably exceeding the entire on-chip SRAM budget. Tile-and-reload schemes can hide this gap, but they amortize DDR traffic poorly when feature maps are produced and consumed in raster order; a streaming row-ring data path that resident-buffers only a few rows at a time is the natural alternative, and it is the second narrative thread (B) of this work.

Taken together, the two constraints — chronic shallow-layer PE underutilization and the impossibility of resident-buffering a large input image on edge FPGA SRAM — define FLUX_CNN's design starting point. The remainder of this section reviews how prior approaches address each constraint individually (§1.2), describes the combined approach that FLUX_CNN takes (§1.3), enumerates the resulting contributions (§1.4), and lays out the structure of the paper (§1.5).

### 1.2 Limitations of Existing Approaches

Existing work that attempts to recover PE utilization or to scale to large inputs on resource-bounded FPGAs falls broadly into three families: hardware-reconfigurable arrays that adapt their interconnect to the layer shape, layer-pipelined or layer-fused designs that keep multiple layers' feature maps simultaneously resident on chip, and fully-unrolled im2col-style mappings that materialize K×K·Cin column vectors into general matrix multiplications. Each family provides one answer to the underutilization problem, but none simultaneously satisfies the two FLUX_CNN constraints — fixed-array simplicity and bounded on-chip footprint for arbitrary H×W inputs.

The first family, hardware-reconfigurable arrays, includes MAERI [MAERI-ASPLOS18], whose Augmented Reduction Tree (ART) network rewires multiplier outputs to handle layers with mismatched shapes, and Eyeriss-v2 [Eyeriss-v2-JETCAS19], whose hierarchical mesh NoC can be reconfigured to feed PE clusters under varying Cin/Cout shapes. Both designs recover utilization across diverse layer geometries, but they pay for that flexibility in RTL complexity: the reconfigurable interconnect itself constitutes a substantial area and timing burden, and verification of the reconfiguration logic is a significant ongoing cost. FLUX_CNN takes the opposite stance — keep the interconnect fixed and shift the burden to the compiler.

The second family, layer-pipelined or layer-fused designs, includes Alwani et al.'s fused-layer accelerator [Alwani-MICRO16], which keeps inter-layer feature maps in on-chip buffers to eliminate up to 95% of off-chip traffic, and the more recent AoCStream work by Kang [Kang-AoCStream-arXiv22, Kang-Sensors23], which pipelines per-layer streaming modules at line-buffer granularity. These designs achieve excellent on-chip data reuse, but they assume that several layers' feature maps can be co-resident on chip — an assumption that does not hold on a 1.6 MB BRAM budget for VGA-scale inputs, regardless of layer count. The closely related Full-Stack Streaming work of Liu et al. [Liu-FullStack-TNNLS21] reaches 97% MAC efficiency on a much larger Arria 10 GX1150 (10× the BRAM of XC7K325T), reinforcing that this family scales with on-chip memory rather than around it.

The third family, fully-unrolled im2col mappings exemplified by cuDNN [cuDNN-arXiv14], lifts every K×K·Cin window into an explicit column of a GEMM, recovering full array utilization through dense matrix multiply. This is the right answer when DRAM bandwidth is plentiful and a large unified buffer is available, but on edge FPGAs the materialized im2col tensor is several times larger than the original feature map, and the resulting BRAM blow-up is precisely what edge platforms cannot absorb.

FLUX_CNN therefore selects a fourth path. Hardware is kept simple and fixed at 16×16 INT8; the compiler remaps shallow-layer convolutions so that the array is filled at run time without any RTL change; and a row-level streaming data path replaces tile-and-reload to bound on-chip footprint at a few thousand bytes regardless of input height and width. The next section makes this approach concrete.

### 1.3 Our Approach

The core design choice of FLUX_CNN is to keep a fixed 16×16 INT8 array, fold shallow-layer convolutions onto that array entirely in software via Ky-fold and Space-to-Depth (S2D) compiler passes, and stream feature maps through the pipeline in a row-ring buffer driven by bidirectional row-credit backpressure, so that a single host-issued `start` runs an arbitrary H×W image to completion. Figure 1 [TBD: Fig.1 顶层框图占位 — 4 模块主链 + side-feed wgt_buffer + DMA 子系统 + 多核 wrapper，§4.1 详述] shows the resulting system: a four-stage decentralized valid-ready main pipeline (`line_buffer → mac_array → parf_accum → ofb_writer`) with `wgt_buffer` feeding weights side-on into `mac_array` from the WRF, a DMA subsystem built on top of a Xilinx AXI DataMover IP, and an optional multi-core wrapper that scales horizontally on the W dimension.

Narrative A — compiler-side PE utilization — is realized by two compiler passes that together cover the shallow-layer regime. Ky-fold targets layers with Cin < 16 by folding the Ky kernel dimension into a virtual cin_fake dimension of width groups_y · Cin, with input data replicated along the y-axis and weights reshaped accordingly; the array sees a normal convolution at full width and is unaware of the transformation [Ky-fold from contributions.md C2.1]. S2D targets layers with stride ≥ 2 by rearranging the stride² spatial phases into Cin, producing an equivalent stride-1 convolution at width stride²·Cin without data replication, which is friendly to DDR bandwidth [S2D from contributions.md C2.2]. Both passes leave the RTL untouched.

Narrative B — system-side support — is realized by a row-level streaming ring and an optional multi-core W-slice scaling. The IFB and OFB SRAMs are addressed modulo strip_rows so that only a small window of rows is resident at any time; for VGA 480×640 with strip_rows = 8 this requires roughly 10 KB of ring storage <!-- 来自 contributions.md C1.2 / README.md -->, three orders of magnitude below the 4.9 MB single-image footprint. A multi-core wrapper splits the W dimension across N cores with computed redundancy halos, and N=4 closes timing on XC7K325T with 20/20 W-slice regression cases passing bit-exactly <!-- 来自 contributions.md C3.7 -->.

A third element, narrative C, supports A and B at the implementation level: each module in the core pipeline maintains its own counters and synchronizes with neighbors through valid-ready handshakes, with no central scheduler. We treat this as an organizing principle rather than a top-line claim — the formal language of buffers, counters, and handshakes was already given by Pellauer et al. [Buffets-ASPLOS19], and FLUX_CNN's contribution at this level is the concrete five-module RTL realization. The next section enumerates the resulting concrete contributions.

### 1.4 Contributions

The contributions of this paper are organized along narrative A (compiler-side PE utilization, primary axis) and narrative B (row-streaming and multi-core, system support), with each contribution backed by either bit-exact regression evidence or, where applicable, quantified resource and cycle-count data. We deliberately keep each claim quantified and bound its scope; where measurements are still pending, we mark the figure with [CHECK].

(1) **Ky-fold compiler pass for fixed-array shallow-layer PE utilization.** We introduce a compiler transformation that, when Cin < 16, folds the Ky kernel dimension onto a virtual cin_fake dimension by replicating inputs along the y-axis and reshaping weights, with no RTL change to the 16×16 array. On the shallow ResNet-18-style layers identified in §1.1 (Cin = 4 or Cin = 8), this raises spatial PE utilization from 12.5%–50% toward the array width [CHECK: 实测 PE 利用率百分比待 fold-mode 回归 cycle count 反算，contributions.md §8.1 #1, #2]. Our literature search for "Ky-fold compiler pass on fixed FPGA arrays" did not return a direct prior art [CHECK: S2D / Ky-fold 在加速器领域引用谱系待 reviewer 阶段补查，contributions.md §8.1 #15].

(2) **Space-to-Depth compiler pass for stride ≥ 2 convolutions.** We introduce a second compiler transformation that, for stride ≥ 2 layers, rearranges stride² spatial phases into Cin without data replication, yielding an equivalent stride-1 convolution at increased Cin width and enabling the standard sliding-window reuse path inside the array. The transformation is purely a compile-time reshape; DDR traffic is preserved rather than inflated <!-- 来自 contributions.md C2.2 -->. The mathematical underpinning of the rearrangement is borrowed from the sub-pixel CNN literature [Shi-CVPR16], but applying it as a compiler pass over an arbitrary pre-trained convolution — without retraining — is, to our knowledge, distinct from the training-time use in the original work.

(3) **Streaming row-ring data path with bidirectional row-credit backpressure.** The IFB, weight, and OFB buffers cooperate as a row-level ring: the line buffer launches a tile only when sufficient input rows are available, and the ODMA returns row-consumption credits to the OFB writer to release ring slots. As a consequence, a single VGA 480×640 image streams through a single core in roughly 10 KB of on-chip ring storage <!-- 来自 contributions.md C1.2 -->. Across 22 ResNet-18-style chained cases plus 24 robustness corner cases (kernel sizes K ∈ {1,2,3,5,7}, strides 1–4, image dimensions from 1×1 up to 120×68), all 46 cases pass bit-exactly within 100k cycles <!-- 来自 contributions.md §4.2 / STATUS.md §2.5 -->.

(4) **Multi-core W-slice scaling with computed redundancy halos.** We split the W dimension across N homogeneous cores using an asymmetric pad / overlap-halo scheme; each core reads its own W stripe directly from DDR with a per-row stride decoupled from the per-burst length, and each core's ODMA writes back to the corresponding W stripe, where DDR concatenation produces the final tensor. The N = 4 configuration closes timing on XC7K325T at the same Fmax as the single core, and 20 of 20 W-slice regression cases (covering K ∈ {1,3,5,7}, stride ∈ {1,2}, W ∈ {8, 32, 33}, single- and multi-layer) pass bit-exactly <!-- 来自 contributions.md C3.5 / C3.7 / STATUS §2.8 -->.

(5) **PyTorch nn.Sequential end-to-end compilation.** A small Python compiler maps PyTorch `Conv2d`/`Sequential` chains to descriptor-driven hardware configurations and DDR feature-map layouts, and a chained-CASE DSL composes 11-layer ResNet-18-style blocks in a few lines. The resulting end-to-end network reaches 86.6% network-level MAC utilization in 593k cycles on a single core [CHECK: end-to-end Wall_us 实测列待重跑取数，contributions.md §8.1 #5], with one identified critical-path issue (the SDP quantization combinational chain limits Fmax to 68.4 MHz on XC7K325T, below the 100 MHz design target) that we document and place on a roadmap rather than fix in this work [CHECK: contributions.md C1.5 + STATUS.md §4 fix path]. We adopt the convention "with documented optimization roadmap" throughout the paper to avoid over-claiming on items that are known but not yet addressed.

These five contributions are detailed in §4–§7. The grouping is itself a writing decision — alternative organizations (e.g., merging (1) and (2) into a single "compiler-side PE utilization" contribution) are equally defensible [TBD: 5 条 vs 4 条 vs 6 条由用户最终裁决].

## 2. Background and Motivation

### 2.1 Spatial Array Dataflows: WS / OS / RS Taxonomy

Spatial accelerators for convolution are conventionally classified by which tensor remains stationary inside the array during a steady-state cycle. The three canonical choices are weight-stationary (WS), output-stationary (OS), and row-stationary (RS); the choice determines which operand pays the bandwidth cost of refresh and which operand benefits from intra-array reuse. We adopt this taxonomy as terminology for the rest of the paper, since the design decisions in §4 and the comparisons in §3 are most naturally expressed against it.

The TPU v1 [TPU-ISCA17] is the canonical WS design: a 256×256 INT8 systolic grid in which weights are pre-loaded into PEs and held while activations stream through, amortizing weight movement across many output pixels. Eyeriss [Eyeriss-ISCA16] is the canonical RS design: a 14×12 PE fabric in which a row of the convolution kernel is held inside each PE row and partial sums diagonally accumulate, exploiting reuse along all three of the kernel, input, and output axes simultaneously. ShiDianNao [ShiDianNao-ISCA15] is a representative OS design at smaller scale, in which each PE retains an output pixel's running partial sum and consumes streamed weight/activation pairs until the output is complete.

FLUX_CNN's 16×16 INT8 array adopts a hybrid that is closest to OS: each column is dedicated to a distinct Cout channel and accumulates its partial sum locally inside `parf_accum`; all 16 columns share the same 16-channel activation vector `act_in_vec` (a column-broadcast of activations across one pixel position), while within each column the 16 PEs correspond to distinct Cin channels and read per-PE weights from the WRF <!-- 来自 RTL/mac_array.sv L36-58 + docs/modules/mac_array.md L25-33,96-99 + docs/modules/mac_col.md L24,33,60 -->. This "column-broadcast activation, per-column output" arrangement maps naturally onto a row-ring streaming feature-map path, because activations are produced and consumed in raster order and outputs are written back per Cout-tile rather than per K×K window. The choice is deliberate — among the three canonical dataflows, OS introduces the least coupling between the array and the line buffer, and is therefore the easiest to combine with row-level streaming.

NVDLA [NVDLA] organizes its convolution datapath as a chain of named stages — CDMA, CMAC, CACC, and SDP — each of which corresponds to a separable concern (data movement, multiplication, accumulation, and post-processing). FLUX_CNN inherits this naming lineage in the form `line_buffer → mac_array → parf_accum → ofb_writer` plus an SDP block, which makes the per-stage decomposition recognizable to readers familiar with NVDLA. With the dataflow vocabulary in place, the next subsection turns to the quantitative pathology that motivates narrative A — the chronic underutilization of a 16-wide array on shallow ResNet layers.

### 2.2 PE Utilization Pathology in Shallow Layers

A 16×16 spatial array sized for the bulk of a CNN systematically underutilizes its PEs on the shallow layers of ResNet-18-style networks, where the input-channel count Cin is much smaller than the array width. This is the central quantitative motivation for narrative A: the layers that are most expensive in raw cycle count, because their H×W is largest, are precisely the layers at which the array runs at a fraction of its nominal throughput. Closing this gap on the shallow tail without touching the array RTL is the design problem that the compiler passes in §5 are constructed to solve.

A per-layer breakdown on a representative ResNet-18-style network makes the pathology concrete. Layer 1 (Cin = 4, K = 7) populates only 4 of the 16 column lanes with non-zero activations per cycle, yielding 12.5% spatial PE utilization; Layers 3 and 4 (Cin = 8) reach 25%; the early tiles of Layer 5 (Cin = 8, Cout = 16) reach 50%; and only from Layer 5b onward, when Cin saturates the array width at 16, does utilization reach 100% <!-- 来自 contributions.md §4.4 / model_analysis.md §2 -->. The terminal fully-connected layers FC_xy (Cout = 2) sit at the opposite boundary, with 2 of 16 column lanes active and the remaining columns idle [CHECK: post-Ky-fold 实测百分比待 fold-mode 回归 cycle count 反算，contributions.md §8.1 #1, #2].

Two distinct utilization quantities recur in the paper, and we keep them separate to avoid confusion. "Spatial PE utilization" refers to the fraction of mac_array PE lanes doing useful work in a steady-state cycle of a single layer, which is the quantity bounded above by Cin / 16 in the unfolded shallow regime; "network-level MAC utilization" refers to the total useful MACs over the total cycles of an end-to-end multi-layer run, which additionally absorbs IDMA / WDMA stalls, layer-boundary refills, and tail effects. The text in §7.4 ties the two together, and §7.5 reports the network-level number on an 11-layer chain.

Layers with very small Cout — most prominently FC_xy with Cout = 2 — are not addressed by the compiler passes in §5. The hardware deliberately leaves the corresponding 14 column lanes idle rather than introduce a column-folding mechanism, because the cycle cost of such layers in a ResNet-18-style network is negligible relative to the cycle cost of the early convolutional layers <!-- 来自 contributions.md C2.4 / model_analysis.md -->. We treat this as a deliberate scope decision rather than a missed optimization, on the grounds that the marginal RTL complexity of a Cout-folding mechanism is not justified by the cycle-count savings it would yield on the target workload.

The shallow-layer PE utilization constraint is now established. The next subsection turns to a second constraint that arises from the same edge-FPGA setting but is largely orthogonal — the on-chip SRAM footprint required to hold a single image of realistic resolution.

### 2.3 Streaming vs Tiled: Memory Footprint of Large-Image Inference

Edge FPGAs in the 7-Series mid-range present a hard ceiling on resident feature-map storage that is not relaxed by clever interconnect or compiler tricks. The Xilinx XC7K325T provides 445 BRAM36 tiles, equivalent to roughly 1.6 MB of on-chip SRAM, of which an FLUX_CNN core already commits 128 BRAM36 to weight, input, shortcut, and partial-sum buffers <!-- 来自 contributions.md §4.1 / STATUS.md line 32-44 -->. A single VGA-resolution 480×640 INT8 input image at 16 channels occupies approximately 4.9 MB, which is roughly three times the entire BRAM budget of the device — the platform simply cannot host the image as a flat resident tensor.

A row-ring streaming data path resolves this constraint by construction. The IFB and OFB SRAMs are addressed modulo a small `strip_rows` parameter, so that at any instant only a window of rows — sized to the K×K convolution receptive field plus a margin — needs to be co-resident with the array. For the VGA case at strip_rows = 8 and W = 640, the resident ring footprint is roughly 8 × 640 = 5120 bytes per channel and on the order of 10 KB total across the active channels <!-- 来自 contributions.md C1.2 -->, three orders of magnitude below the 4.9 MB flat-tensor footprint. Streaming is therefore not an optimization that one could choose to skip on this platform; it is the only data path that fits.

This positions FLUX_CNN against two related families of streaming designs. Alwani et al. [Alwani-MICRO16] keep multiple layers' feature maps simultaneously resident in on-chip buffers to eliminate inter-layer DDR traffic, and Kang's AoCStream [Kang-AoCStream-arXiv22, Kang-Sensors23] pipelines per-layer line buffers across a fully-on-chip dataflow. Both achieve excellent inter-layer reuse, but both assume that several layers' worth of feature maps can be co-resident — an assumption that does not hold on a 1.6 MB BRAM budget for VGA-scale inputs. FLUX_CNN instead streams a single layer at a time and allows DDR to act as the inter-layer buffer; §3.4 returns to the precise comparison.

The streaming constraint is orthogonal to the PE-utilization constraint of §2.2 — one concerns spatial occupancy of the array, the other concerns the temporal residency of feature-map data — but both arise from the same edge-FPGA hardware envelope. The next subsection rolls the two constraints up into a compact set of design goals that the architecture and compiler chapters will then realize. [TBD: 是否在此处插入 SRAM-vs-image-size 曲线图作为 Fig.2，待用户决定]

### 2.4 Design Goals

We now consolidate the two motivations of §2.2 and §2.3 into a small set of design goals that organize the architecture, compiler, and system-integration choices in the rest of the paper. These goals deliberately frame what FLUX_CNN must deliver, without yet committing to the specific mechanisms — the mechanisms are introduced in §4–§6 and are positioned against prior art in §3.

Three goals follow directly from the constraints. (a) Hardware simplicity: the spatial array remains a fixed 16×16 INT8 grid with a non-reconfigurable interconnect, on the grounds that reconfigurable PE networks pay a substantial RTL and verification cost that is not justified when the same effect can be obtained at compile time <!-- 来自 contributions.md C1.1 -->. (b) Compiler-side PE filling: shallow-layer underutilization is recovered by compiler transformations that reshape the convolution onto a fully-occupied 16-wide array, with no runtime reconfiguration <!-- 来自 contributions.md C2.1 / C2.2 -->. (c) Single-shot row-ring streaming: a single host-issued `start` runs an arbitrary H×W image through the pipeline, with the on-chip ring sized only to the receptive-field margin <!-- 来自 contributions.md C1.2 -->.

These three goals correspond, respectively, to three distinct positions in the prior-art space: fixed-array designs with a lineage from TPU and Gemmini; compiler-only PE-utilization recovery as opposed to the hardware-reconfigurable ART/NoC route; and row-level streaming as opposed to layer-fused or fully-tiled approaches. The next chapter walks through these three families in turn and identifies the specific points at which FLUX_CNN diverges from each.

## 3. Related Work

### 3.1 Spatial Array Accelerators

Spatial array accelerators for convolution form a now well-populated design space, and we organize related work along three axes that are most useful for positioning FLUX_CNN: array scale (PE count), dataflow choice (WS / OS / RS), and target platform (ASIC vs. FPGA). These three axes are largely orthogonal, and the works cited in this section together cover most of their joint space; we use them as coordinates rather than as a chronological survey.

The TPU v1 [TPU-ISCA17] anchors the large-ASIC, weight-stationary, datacenter end of the space with a 256×256 INT8 systolic grid and 28 MiB of unified buffer. Eyeriss [Eyeriss-ISCA16, Eyeriss-JSSC17] occupies a smaller-ASIC, row-stationary niche (14×12 PEs, 65 nm) and is the canonical embedded RS reference. ShiDianNao [ShiDianNao-ISCA15] sits in the small-image, sensor-coupled OS corner. Gemmini [Gemmini-DAC21] and NVDLA [NVDLA] both target configurable on-SoC inference IP; Gemmini is open-source and parametric (with a 16×16 INT8 instance that is the most direct hardware analogue to FLUX_CNN), while NVDLA is an industrial reference design with a CDMA → CMAC → CACC → SDP pipeline. Simba [Simba-MICRO19] occupies the multi-chiplet end of the space, with 36 chiplets each carrying 128 MAC and ground-referenced signaling between them. Together these works span the array-scale dimension by roughly three orders of magnitude.

FLUX_CNN's 16×16 INT8 array is closest in scale to a single Gemmini instance and to a single Simba chiplet, but it differs from both in dataflow and in control style: it adopts an OS-with-column-broadcast hybrid (each column owns one Cout channel, all columns share one 16-channel activation vector) <!-- 来自 contributions.md C1.1 + RTL/mac_array.sv -->, and it replaces a central scheduler with the decentralized valid-ready handshake described in §4.2. We treat the choice of OS-with-column-broadcast not as a novel dataflow but as the dataflow most compatible with row-level streaming, since under OS the array consumes activations in raster order and emits outputs per Cout-tile.

The naming convention `line_buffer → mac_array → parf_accum → ofb_writer`, plus an SDP block, deliberately mirrors NVDLA's CDMA → CMAC → CACC → SDP decomposition, so that a reader familiar with NVDLA can map FLUX_CNN's per-stage concerns onto an established vocabulary [NVDLA]. The next subsection turns to the first of the two design choices that distinguish FLUX_CNN from this baseline lineage: how to recover PE utilization on layers whose Cin is below the array width.

### 3.2 Hardware-Reconfigurable Approaches to PE Utilization

A first family of responses to shape-mismatch between the network and the array introduces hardware-reconfigurable interconnect, on the grounds that a PE network that can rewire itself can absorb arbitrary layer geometries. We summarize three representative works along this axis and contrast them with the compiler-only stance taken by FLUX_CNN.

MAERI [MAERI-ASPLOS18] equips its multiplier array with an Augmented Reduction Tree (ART) and a Distribution Tree, both of which are runtime-configurable, and reports PE utilization gains of 8–459% across diverse mappings versus a rigid-NoC baseline. Eyeriss-v2 [Eyeriss-v2-JETCAS19] generalizes the original RS dataflow with a hierarchical mesh NoC whose multicast / unicast / broadcast modes are reconfigured per layer, and demonstrates 12.6× speedup and 2.5× energy efficiency over Eyeriss v1 on sparse MobileNet. Tangram [Tangram-ASPLOS19] takes the same reconfiguration philosophy to the inter-layer level by alternating buffer-sharing dataflows across tiled accelerator instances, achieving a 2× performance and 45% energy improvement on multi-layer pipelines.

FLUX_CNN occupies the opposite point of this design choice. The interconnect inside the 16×16 array is fixed — column-broadcast activations and per-column independent Cout accumulation — and the recovery of shallow-layer PE utilization is delegated entirely to two compiler passes (Ky-fold and S2D, §5) that reshape the convolution before it reaches the RTL. The trade-off is real: hardware-reconfigurable approaches absorb a wider envelope of layer shapes but pay an RTL-area and verification cost, while compiler-only approaches keep the RTL minimal at the cost of a per-layer transformation step. The two stances are not mutually exclusive — one could in principle combine them — but the FLUX_CNN argument is that for the shallow ResNet-18-style regime the compiler side alone suffices, and the saved RTL complexity is a meaningful engineering benefit on a mid-range FPGA.

This positions FLUX_CNN against the hardware-reconfigurable family as a complementary point, not a contradicting one. The next subsection moves into the language in which compiler-side approaches are usually expressed — loop-nest formalisms and mapping search — and identifies the precise gap that Ky-fold and S2D fill within that language.

### 3.3 Compiler / Loop-Nest Co-Design

Compiler-side and loop-nest co-design forms the natural sibling family to hardware-reconfigurable interconnect: instead of changing the wires at runtime, one changes the loop nest at compile time. We summarize the most relevant works in this family and locate the position that Ky-fold and S2D occupy within them.

Timeloop [Timeloop-ISPASS19] formalizes a DNN dataflow as a mapping over a 7-deep loop nest and provides a search engine that, paired with Accelergy, evaluates each candidate mapping for energy and performance. Interstellar [Interstellar-ASPLOS20] expresses the same loop nest in Halide scheduling primitives and provides a formal taxonomy of existing dense DNN accelerators, reporting up to 4.2× CNN energy improvement at fixed throughput. TVM and its FPGA-targeted backend VTA [TVM-OSDI18, VTA-MICRO19] productize loop-level scheduling into an end-to-end compiler stack; VTA's reference 256-PE @ 100 MHz Zynq instance is the most direct open-source compiler-stack analogue to FLUX_CNN. Ma et al.'s loop-optimization study [Ma-FPGA17] systematically enumerates tiling, unrolling, and interchange decisions on a 4–6-dimensional loop nest, and reaches 645 GOPS on Arria 10 GX1150 for VGG-16. Finally, cuDNN's im2col [cuDNN-arXiv14] represents the fully-unrolled extreme of this language: every K×K·Cin window is materialized as a column of an explicit GEMM.

Within this language the Ky-fold pass is most naturally read as a partial im2col that folds only the Ky dimension into Cin, replicating inputs along the y-axis and reshaping weights, while leaving Kx as a time-multiplexed axis. This sits between cuDNN's full im2col (which inflates memory by K² and is impractical at the FPGA scale) and a per-layer mapping search (which keeps the original loop nest intact and accepts the Cin/16 underutilization as given). The S2D pass occupies a different position again: the rearrangement of stride² spatial phases into Cin is purely a compile-time reshape and adds no replication. Both passes sit inside the language of loop transformations but are, to our knowledge, not directly named as such in the surveyed works [CHECK: S2D 在加速器领域的引用谱系待 reviewer 阶段补查 ASPLOS/HPCA 是否有 architecture-side S2D 更早出处，contributions.md §8.1 #15]. The mathematical underpinning of S2D's spatial-phase rearrangement is the inverse of the sub-pixel convolution layer of Shi et al. [Shi-CVPR16], but Shi et al. apply it as a learned upsampling operator at training time, whereas FLUX_CNN applies it as a compile-time reshape on a pre-trained, unmodified convolution.

The closest direct comparison within this family is Liu et al.'s Full-Stack streaming accelerator [Liu-FullStack-TNNLS21], which reaches 97% MAC efficiency on Arria 10 GX1150 by combining streaming with residual fusion and aggressive DSP utilization. We will return to this work in §3.4, since its system-level concerns are closer to narrative B than to narrative A; but the contrast is informative here too — Liu et al. attain near-peak MAC efficiency on a much larger device with full layer-fusion, while FLUX_CNN attains 86.6% network-level MAC utilization on a 16× smaller mid-range FPGA <!-- 来自 contributions.md §4.3 / STATUS.md line 355 --> with single-layer-at-a-time execution and compiler-side filling. The two designs sit at different points of a scale-vs-complexity Pareto frontier rather than at the same point. The next subsection moves to the streaming family proper, which is the second principal axis along which FLUX_CNN is positioned.

### 3.4 Streaming / Line-Buffer FPGA Accelerators

The streaming / line-buffer family is the most directly comparable family of related work to FLUX_CNN's narrative B, and three works within it — Alwani et al.'s fused-layer accelerator, Kang's AoCStream, and Liu et al.'s Full-Stack streaming accelerator — represent the closest prior art on row-streaming and inter-layer feature-map management. We treat this subsection as the principal site for differentiation, and we are deliberate about not over-claiming on points that are already covered by these works.

The shared substrate of this family is a line-buffer-driven dataflow in which feature maps are produced and consumed in raster order, with the on-chip buffer sized to the receptive field rather than to the full feature map. fpgaConvNet [fpgaConvNet-FCCM16, fpgaConvNet-TNNLS19] introduces a synchronous-dataflow formulation in which each layer becomes a streaming hardware module and HLS generates the full per-layer pipeline; it reports a 2.94× performance-density gain over earlier FPGA-based ConvNet architectures. Snowflake [Snowflake-ISCAS17] runs a line-buffer-driven vector engine on Zynq XC7Z045 with 91% average computational efficiency on AlexNet, GoogLeNet, and ResNet-50, and is the most direct same-platform reference point for §7. Angel-Eye [Angel-Eye-TCAD18] adds INT8 quantization-aware mapping on the same Zynq-class device, with end-to-end Caffe → hardware compilation. Aydonat et al.'s DLA [Aydonat-FPGA17] adopts a 1-D Winograd-accelerated streaming engine on Arria 10 and reaches 1382 GFLOPS on AlexNet, while Lu et al.'s Winograd FPGA accelerator [LuWinograd-FCCM17] reports 854.6 GOPS on ZCU102. These six works collectively establish the streaming line-buffer mode and the per-layer-module composition that is the de facto baseline for FPGA CNN accelerators on Xilinx and Intel platforms.

Within this established mode, Alwani et al.'s fused-layer accelerator [Alwani-MICRO16] is the clearest threat to FLUX_CNN's row-streaming claim, because it operates at sub-feature-map granularity and explicitly targets inter-layer on-chip retention. Alwani et al. fuse the first 5 conv layers of VGGNet-E into a single on-chip pipeline, eliminating 95% of the inter-layer DRAM traffic at a cost of 362 KB on-chip storage. The differentiation point for FLUX_CNN is granularity and resource regime: Alwani et al. retain multiple layers' feature-map slices simultaneously on chip, requiring on-chip storage that scales with the number of fused layers, whereas FLUX_CNN runs one layer at a time on a single shared 16×16 core, with only a small `strip_rows`-sized window of the current layer's feature map resident, and with DDR acting as the inter-layer buffer. On a 1.6 MB BRAM budget for VGA-scale inputs, the Alwani regime does not fit; FLUX_CNN's row-level granularity is therefore not a strict improvement over fused-layer execution but a different point on the streaming-vs-fusion design space, suited to smaller devices and longer networks.

Kang's AoCStream [Kang-AoCStream-arXiv22, Kang-Sensors23] is the second close prior art and the work whose stream-based line-buffer is most architecturally similar to FLUX_CNN's IFB ring. AoCStream targets low-end FPGAs and uses dedicated per-layer line-buffer modules whose size scales linearly with image width rather than quadratically with image area, with the explicit goal of fitting an entire CNN object detector on chip without external memory. The differentiation here is layer-pipelined-multi-block versus layer-serial-single-core, and the position taken on external memory: FLUX_CNN's single 16×16 core is shared across all layers in time, with row-level credits and DDR as the inter-layer buffer, whereas AoCStream instantiates per-layer hardware blocks and uses on-chip-only data flow. The "all-on-chip" assumption is well-suited to the small detection networks Kang targets but does not extend to the larger networks at VGA-scale resolution that FLUX_CNN must support; conversely, AoCStream achieves higher peak throughput at the cost of more replicated MAC hardware.

Liu et al.'s Full-Stack streaming accelerator [Liu-FullStack-TNNLS21] is the third close prior art and the most directly comparable in algorithmic feature support. Liu et al. unify conv and deconv layers into a streaming module, support residual and concatenative connections (covering both ResNet and DenseNet), and report 97% MAC efficiency at >1.3 TOP/s throughput on Arria 10 GX1150. The differentiation here is principally one of scale rather than approach: Arria 10 GX1150 has roughly an order of magnitude more BRAM and DSP than XC7K325T <!-- 来自 contributions.md §5.3 -->, which enables Liu et al.'s aggressive layer fusion and DSP packing; FLUX_CNN's 86.6% network-level MAC utilization on a 16× smaller mid-range FPGA <!-- 来自 contributions.md §4.3 --> is the comparable number on the smaller device, and it is reached without layer fusion. We read this comparison as a same-philosophy point on a different Pareto frontier rather than as a head-to-head comparison.

Snowflake and Angel-Eye remain the closest same-device peers (XC7Z045 / XC7Z020 Zynq parts, comparable to XC7K325T in BRAM and DSP class) and we will report quantitative comparison numbers against them in §7.6 [CHECK: 同器件 Snowflake / Angel-Eye Fmax / 资源 / 整网 MAC% 对照数字待 reviewer 阶段补查 contributions.md §8.1 #10–13]. The next subsection briefly addresses the algorithmic-side concerns — quantization and post-processing fusion — that frame FLUX_CNN's SDP block.

### 3.5 Quantization and Fusion

INT8 quantization and post-processing fusion (bias / scale / shift / ReLU / clip / residual) are by now standard ingredients of edge CNN accelerators, and we touch on them only as background to FLUX_CNN's SDP block; the principal contributions of this paper lie elsewhere.

On the algorithmic side, Jacob et al.'s quantization-aware training framework [Jacob-CVPR18] established the now-conventional per-channel asymmetric INT8 scheme that maintains within 1% accuracy of FP32 on ImageNet-class benchmarks, and provides the precondition under which FLUX_CNN's INT8 MAC array operates. He et al.'s ResNet [ResNet-CVPR16] introduced the residual connection that FLUX_CNN's SDP block is built to fuse natively. NVDLA [NVDLA] codified the SDP (Single Data Point processor) pipeline stage as a separate post-MAC unit handling bias, BN, ReLU, and quantization, and FLUX_CNN inherits both the name and the per-stage decomposition. EIE [EIE-ISCA16] and ESE [ESE-FPGA17] extend the quantization line to sparse-plus-quantized inference, a direction that FLUX_CNN does not pursue; we cite them to acknowledge the orthogonal sparsity axis.

FLUX_CNN's contribution within this fusion family is incremental rather than novel. The R.1 refactor relocated bias from the mac_array to the SDP, removing the previous 32-Cout-slice limit on the bias_rf <!-- 来自 contributions.md C1.4 / memory/bias_to_sdp_refactor.md -->; the R.2 refactor added programmable `shortcut_mult` / `shortcut_shift` quantization factors to the residual path, allowing the residual contribution to be re-quantized to match the main path's scale. We surface these as system-completeness arguments in §6.3 rather than as headline contributions, on the grounds that the broader SDP fusion concept is well established. ### 3.6 Positioning of FLUX_CNN

We close this chapter by placing FLUX_CNN explicitly along the five axes that the previous five subsections have introduced, so that the niche claimed by this paper is unambiguous and so that the differentiation arguments of §3.2–§3.4 can be read as a single coordinate rather than as five separate negations.

The five axes are: (1) intra-array dataflow, on which FLUX_CNN is OS-with-column-broadcast and per-column PARF, anchored against TPU v1's WS, Eyeriss's RS, and ShiDianNao's small-image OS; (2) the location of PE-utilization recovery, on which FLUX_CNN is compiler-only Ky-fold + S2D, anchored against MAERI's ART, Eyeriss-v2's reconfigurable mesh NoC, and Tangram's inter-layer reconfiguration; (3) streaming granularity, on which FLUX_CNN is row-level with bidirectional row-credit backpressure, anchored against Alwani's layer-fused multi-layer-resident scheme, Kang's per-layer-block all-on-chip stream, and Liu et al.'s layer-fused streaming on a much larger device; (4) the multi-core scaling dimension, on which FLUX_CNN splits the W dimension with computed redundancy halos, anchored against Simba's chiplet-level channel split; and (5) the scope of the compiler stack, on which FLUX_CNN is a hand-rolled mini-compiler with a fixed backend, anchored against TVM/VTA's general stack, fpgaConvNet's HLS templates, and Vitis AI's vendor toolchain.

The differentiation arguments developed in §3.2–§3.4 each correspond to one of these axes: §3.2 on axis (2), §3.3 on axes (2) and (5), and §3.4 on axes (3) and (4). ## 4. Architecture

This chapter develops the architecture along axis (1) of the five-axis frame established in §3.5, and grounds two of the lower-novelty but load-bearing engineering contributions, C1.1 (decentralized valid-ready pipeline) and C1.2 (streaming row-ring datapath), together with the per-column PARF organisation (C1.3) and the seven-deep loop nest that the compiler in §5 targets (C2.4). We have deliberately kept the architecture conventional in places where novelty would not justify the verification cost; the design choices that we do claim are flagged at the section level rather than buried in microarchitectural lists.

### 4.1 Top-Level Architecture

FLUX_CNN is organised as two layers: a five-module **core pipeline** that performs the convolution itself, and a **DMA subsystem** that bridges the core to external memory and to the host. From the outside, the accelerator exposes only one AXI4 master port (for off-chip data movement) and one AXI-Lite slave port (for descriptors and configuration registers), which keeps the SoC integration boundary deliberately narrow.

The core pipeline is the chain `line_buffer → mac_array → parf_accum → ofb_writer`, with `wgt_buffer` feeding weights side-on into `mac_array` from the WRF rather than sitting in the main forward path. Around the core, the DMA subsystem comprises three lightweight controllers — `idma_ctrl`, `wdma_ctrl`, and `odma_ctrl` — that drive a Xilinx `axi_dm` DataMover IP (whose MM2S channel is shared between IDMA and WDMA via `mm2s_arb`), an `axi_m_mux` that aggregates DataMover and DFE traffic onto the single external master port, and an `axi_lite_csr` block that exposes the configuration registers and descriptor ingress to the host. <!-- 来自 CLAUDE.md 顶层架构描述 -->

The naming of the core stages — `line_buffer → mac_array → parf_accum → sdp` — is a conscious continuation of the NVDLA `CDMA → CMAC → CACC → SDP` lineage [NVDLA] and of the Snowflake-style streaming convolution decomposition [Snowflake-ISCAS17]. We adopt this taxonomy not because it is mandatory but because it allows readers familiar with either NVDLA or the broader streaming-convolution literature to map FLUX_CNN's blocks onto a shared vocabulary, which makes the more unusual choices (per-column PARF, row-ring backpressure) easier to localise.

The three DMA stages — IDMA, compute, ODMA — are designed to overlap rather than serialise; the explicit timing model and the row-credit machinery that make this overlap possible are deferred to §4.3. The next subsection first treats the inter-module handshake that underpins the entire pipeline.

### 4.2 Decentralized Valid-Ready Pipeline

The five core modules each maintain their own counters (row, column, kx/ky, slice) and communicate exclusively through valid-ready handshakes at module boundaries; there is no central FSM or global scheduler that orchestrates them (C1.1). Each module knows only its local progress and its immediate upstream/downstream readiness, which is the smallest amount of state needed to keep the pipeline elastic.

Concretely, every module-to-module boundary follows the same `counter + ready-backpressure` template: the producer holds `valid` until the consumer's `ready` is high, and the in-flight datum is preserved across stalls. This elastic-join discipline lets us overlap the **drain** of `parf_accum` tile *N* with the **fill** of tile *N+1*, because the first round of fill consumes WRF/IFB tokens while the drain still owns the PSUM read port — the two phases share no state, only handshake credits.

The `sequencer` exists, but its job is restricted to **cross-block kick-off synchronisation** — it dispatches the IDMA / compute / ODMA stages of a tile at the right moment and then steps back; it is not a centralised scheduler that micromanages the pipeline. The engineering evidence that this minimal coordination is sufficient is the 22-case chained regression on a ResNet-18-style workload, which passes bit-exact across all three compilation modes (no-fold, `--fold`, `--fold --s2d`) [CHECK: 22-case chained regression PASS rate, 来自 STATUS.md].

This decentralisation contrasts with three nearby reference points: Gemmini's central RoCC scheduler [Gemmini-DAC21], NVDLA's explicit controller hierarchy [NVDLA], and VTA's four-stage `fetch–load–compute–store` ISA [VTA-Micro19], all of which retain a stronger global controller. Buffets [Buffets-ASPLOS19] supplies the formal `buffer + counter + handshake` vocabulary that we adopt informally; we do not claim a contribution at the level of the Buffets formalism, only that the FLUX_CNN core pipeline is a faithful instance of that pattern at the module-chain level rather than the storage-element level.

The handshake semantics described here are the substrate on which the next subsection's row-level forward-pressure mechanism is built; without elastic backpressure at every boundary, the row-ring would not be able to stall cleanly when downstream credits run out.

### 4.3 Streaming Row-Ring Datapath with Bidirectional Credit Backpressure

The IFB and OFB are physically organised as **row-level ring buffers** addressed modulo `strip_rows`, and the pipeline interlocks at row granularity rather than at frame granularity (C1.2). Specifically, `line_buffer` only emits a sliding window when `rows_available ≥ y_out · stride + Ky` (forward-pressure on the input side), and `ODMA` returns a row-credit to `ofb_writer` after each output row leaves the chip, so the writer is held back when DDR cannot keep up. The `sequencer`, on a single dispatch, simultaneously starts IDMA, compute, and ODMA for a tile, allowing all three stages to run concurrently rather than in lockstep.

Mathematically, `wptr` and `rptr` are modulo counters that never reset — wraparound is implicit in the ring arithmetic, which removes a class of frame-boundary bugs that batch-mode designs typically have to handle explicitly. The capacity argument is concrete: a VGA 480×640 image, nominally 4.9 MB if buffered whole, fits in roughly 10 KB of on-chip ring storage at `strip_rows = 8`, `W = 640` [CHECK: ring footprint 实测]; the rest of the image streams through the ring rather than residing on it. <!-- 来自 CLAUDE.md "streaming row-ring" 描述 -->

The robustness evidence for the row-ring is breadth rather than headline performance: the 22-case ResNet-18-style regression plus the 24-case shape-stress suite — 46 cases in total — pass bit-exact, including 1×1 FC degenerate shapes, 15×17 odd dimensions, and 120×68 large feature maps [CHECK: 24-case shape suite count, 来自 STATUS.md]. The ring is exercised across H, W, K, and stride extremes that the compiler-side passes (§5) all eventually feed into the same hardware path.

We frame "unified streaming row-ring" as the sole runtime mode of FLUX_CNN; the original batch mode is now a degenerate case in which the ring capacity happens to cover the whole feature map. The fine-grained differentiation against layer-level streaming work — Alwani [Alwani-MICRO16], Kang [Kang-Sensors23], and Liu [Liu-TNNLS21] — has been laid out along axis (3) in §3.4 and is not repeated here; this section restricts itself to the hardware realisation of row-level granularity, leaving the comparative narrative in §3.4.

The accumulator unit that sits inside this ring deserves its own subsection, since its internal organisation has consequences for both port pressure and area, which the next subsection takes up.

### 4.4 Per-Column PARF Accumulator

`parf_accum` is **not** a single monolithic PSUM SRAM; instead, it is sixteen `parf_col` instances — one per MAC column — each owning an independent SRAM, all wrapped in a thin shell that broadcasts `wr_addr`, `we`, and `rd_addr` (C1.3). The columns share addressing logic but not storage, and they share schedule but not bandwidth.

This organisation is a direct match for the dataflow: activations are broadcast across columns (output-stationary with column broadcast, axis (1)), while each column independently produces its own `cout` partial sum. Because every column has its own SRAM, each can be implemented with a **single-port BRAM**, and the per-cycle read/write conflict that would otherwise force a multi-port memory disappears at the column boundary [CHECK: parf_col BRAM count and port configuration].

By contrast, NVDLA-class and Gemmini-class designs typically centralise the partial-sum store into a larger SRAM that must then arbitrate between read (drain) and write (accumulate) ports, often pushing the design toward dual-port or banked configurations [NVDLA] [Gemmini-DAC21]. The per-column split releases that port pressure structurally — there is no arbiter at the PSUM boundary because there is no shared PSUM. <!-- 来自 CLAUDE.md parf_accum 描述 -->

We classify this as a **unit-level implementation choice** rather than a top-level contribution; it is one of the engineering details that makes the rest of the pipeline cheap to clock-close, and we report it here for completeness rather than as a novelty claim. ### 4.5 Loop-Nest Realization

The physical 16×16 MAC array handles arbitrary `Cin` and `Cout` through two layers of slicing: `cin_slices = ⌈Cin / 16⌉` is consumed in the **time dimension** by accumulating into PARF across slices, and `cout_slices = ⌈Cout / 16⌉` is consumed by writing successive 16-channel segments **contiguously within an output row** in NHWC order, so that downstream layers see a single concatenated channel axis (C2.4). Neither dimension imposes a hard limit beyond DDR capacity.

Concretely, the hardware loop nest for one layer is seven deep: `for y_out > for cs (cout-slice) > for tile (W-tile) > for cins (cin-slice) > for ky > for kx > for iss_pos`. When a layer's working set exceeds on-chip capacity, the compiler automatically introduces strip slicing along H (selecting `strip_rows`) and tile slicing along W (selecting `tile_cols`), which become the two outermost time-multiplexed dimensions of the same nest rather than separate execution modes.

Two prior-art reference points frame this nest. Interstellar [Interstellar-ASPLOS20] gives a seven-loop formal taxonomy of CNN dataflows that maps essentially one-to-one onto our nest, which we view as confirmation that the structure is well-understood rather than novel. Ma et al. [Ma-FPGA17] is the earlier systematic study of 4–6-deep convolution loop nests on FPGA and provides the same-language baseline. We do not claim a new loop-nest taxonomy; we claim that this particular nest is the **target surface** that the compiler in §5 maps Ky-fold and S2D into.

What this section establishes for the reader is therefore narrow but load-bearing: the hardware runs an **ordinary seven-deep convolution loop nest** with no fold-aware paths, no reconfigurable interconnect, and no special cases for small `Cin`. The next chapter develops the compiler side, where Ky-fold and S2D are shown to be **transformations on the inputs to this same nest** rather than new modes of the nest itself.

## 5. Compiler Optimizations for PE Utilization

A 16×16 fixed MAC array fully fills only when both `Cin ≥ 16` and `Cout ≥ 16`. Real CNN workloads routinely violate the `Cin` half of that condition — the first convolution of an RGB pipeline carries `Cin=3` or `Cin=4`, and a strided downsampler often arrives before channel depth has grown. Rather than reconfigure the array at runtime to match these layers, FLUX_CNN keeps the array fixed and pushes the geometric mismatch into two compiler-side input/weight rearrangements (Ky-fold and Space-to-Depth) that the hardware never observes. This section formalizes both transforms, gives the joint trigger logic, and contrasts the compiler-only route with hardware-reconfigurable alternatives.

> **Metric scope.** Throughout this chapter, "PE utilization" refers to the per-layer occupancy of the `mac_array` during its compute phase. This is *not* the same as the network-level 86.6% MAC% reported in §7.4, which additionally absorbs IDMA/ODMA stalls and cross-layer transitions. The relation between the two metrics is discussed at the end of §7.4.

### 5.1 Ky-Folding

When `Cin < 16` the PE rows starve, and the MAC array degenerates to `Cin/16` row utilization (12.5% for `Cin=2`, 25% for `Cin=4`). Ky-folding repairs this entirely on the compiler side: along the kernel-y dimension we group every `groups_y = HW_PE / Cin` rows and concatenate them into the channel dimension, producing a virtual convolution with `K_new = kyper = ⌈K/groups_y⌉` and `cin_fake = groups_y · Cin ≥ HW_PE`. The hardware sees a normal stride=1 convolution against `cin_fake` channels and is unaware that any folding occurred. <!-- C2.1 -->

Concretely, with `ky = g·kyper + ky_local`, the input and weight tensors are rearranged as `I'[y_virt, x, g·Cin+c] = I_padded[y_virt + g·kyper, x, c]` and `W'[ky_local, kx, co, g·Cin+c] = W[g·kyper + ky_local, kx, co, c]`; pad-out positions (`pad_ky = groups_y · kyper - K`) are zero-filled. The transform is implemented in `toolchain/hw_files.py` as `compute_fold_params`, `fold_input`, and `fold_weights`, mirroring the `psum_reshape` style of small reusable rearrangement passes <!-- 来自 docs/pe-fold.md §1 / hw_files.py -->. No RTL change is required; the hardware path is identical to a vanilla convolution.

The benefit is sharp on shallow-channel layers. ResNet-18-style entry layers with `K=7, Cin=4` jump from `Cin/HW_PE = 25%` row utilization to a fully-packed `cin_fake=16` mapping after Ky-folding [CHECK: per-layer measured PE utilization for Layer 1/3/4]. The cost is a `groups_y`-fold inflation of the IFB footprint along the y-axis, since each virtual row materializes `groups_y` y-shifted physical rows; for `Cin=4, HW_PE=16` this is a 4× IFB inflation, which the streaming row-ring tolerates because the entry-layer feature map is small.

Ky-folding is methodologically related to im2col as used by cuDNN, but is strictly lighter weight: only the `Ky` dimension is unrolled into channels, while `Kx` and the spatial loop remain as ordinary convolution. The full im2col matrix would inflate the input by a factor of `K·K`, materializing all `K²` Kx/Ky positions as separate channels, which on a memory-constrained accelerator is prohibitive. Ky-fold instead exploits the fact that the array is already PE-row parallel along `Cin` and only borrows enough `Ky` slots to fill those rows, leaving the rest of the convolution loop intact.

In broader perspective, Ky-folding represents a compiler-only solution to the same PE-underutilization problem that motivates hardware-reconfigurable fabrics such as MAERI's ART [Kwon@ASPLOS'18] and Eyeriss-v2's hierarchical NoC [Chen@JETCAS'19]. Those proposals add reconfigurable interconnect at synthesis cost; FLUX_CNN extracts equivalent geometric flexibility through input/weight rearrangement at zero hardware cost. To our knowledge, packaging this Ky-only fold as an ahead-of-time pass on a fixed 2-D MAC array, with no runtime configuration and no per-layer microcode, is a distinguishing point of this work; a detailed four-axis comparison is deferred to §5.4.

### 5.2 Space-to-Depth Folding

For convolutions with `stride ≥ 2`, FLUX_CNN applies a Space-to-Depth (S2D) compiler pass that decomposes the kernel into `stride²` phases indexed by `(kx mod stride, ky mod stride)` and folds each phase into the channel dimension. The result is an equivalent stride=1 convolution with `K_new = ⌈K/stride⌉` and `Cin_new = stride² · Cin`, which the array runs as a normal sliding-window convolution. <!-- C2.2 -->

The rearrangement is purely a permutation of input bytes: with `p = a·stride + b` indexing the phase, we have `I'[Y, X, p·Cin+c] = I_padded[Y·stride+a, X·stride+b, c]` and the matching weight reshape, with zero-padding only when `K` is not divisible by `stride` <!-- 来自 docs/pe-fold.md §2 -->. Because no input element is duplicated, S2D is DDR-bandwidth-neutral (in bytes) — it only relocates data on its way into the IFB. After folding, the convolution runs at `stride=1`, which re-enables the ARF `reuse_en=1` sliding-window cache and therefore drops IFB reads sharply on multi-Cout layers; when `K` is divisible by `stride` (e.g. `K=8, stride=2`), `pad_waste = 0` and no MAC is wasted on padding.

The phase-decomposition idea originates with Shi et al.'s sub-pixel convolution layer [Shi@CVPR'16], where it was introduced as a *training-time* layer for super-resolution. The same algebraic identity has since appeared under various names in the deep-learning ecosystem (e.g. as the YOLOv5 "focus" layer, and informally as "pixel-shuffle inverse"). FLUX_CNN's contribution is not the identity itself but its repackaging as a *post-training* compile pass that can be applied transparently to any pre-trained convolution with `stride ≥ 2`, with no retraining and no algorithmic change visible to the model.

Compared with Ky-folding, S2D has a strictly more favorable memory profile: while Ky-folding inflates the IFB by `groups_y`, S2D is an in-place permutation. In multi-core scenarios where DDR bandwidth, not on-chip storage, is the binding constraint, S2D is therefore preferable whenever its trigger condition is met [CHECK: DDR 带宽节省比例待对照 run]. Conversely, S2D requires `stride ≥ 2`, so the two transforms are largely complementary rather than competing.

A note on prior art: while the sub-pixel decomposition itself is well-known, its use as an accelerator compile pass — and in particular its *automatic* application alongside Ky-folding, which we describe next — appears to be less standard in the published accelerator literature. The detailed comparison against hardware-reconfigurable alternatives is deferred to §5.4. [CHECK: S2D 加速器领域引用谱系]

### 5.3 Joint Trigger Logic and Decision Automation

The two folds are not orthogonal: S2D inflates `Cin` by `stride²`, which can lift a layer above the `Cin < 16` threshold and obviate Ky-folding. The compiler therefore evaluates them in a fixed order: first S2D (triggers on `stride ≥ 2 AND K ≥ stride`), then a *re-evaluation* of Ky-folding against the post-S2D channel count (triggers on `K > 1 AND Cin < 16`). In the typical case a strided entry layer has `Cin=3` and `stride=2`, lifting `Cin'` to `12` post-S2D — close to the threshold but still below — so both transforms compose; for `stride=2, Cin=4` the post-S2D channel count `Cin'=16` is exactly at threshold and Ky-folding is suppressed. <!-- C2.3 -->

This decision logic lives entirely in the compiler and is exercised continuously by the regression suite: `run_regression.py` runs all 22 ResNet-18-style cases under three modes — no fold, `--fold` only, and `--fold --s2d` — and all configurations pass bit-exactly against a NumPy reference. Because the hardware is identical across modes, the regression simultaneously validates the bit-exactness of the compiler passes and the hardware's indifference to them. <!-- 来自 toolchain/run_regression.py 与 §7 实测 -->

The complementary case — `Cout < 16` — is intentionally *not* optimized in the current design. Layers such as small classifier heads with `Cout=2` simply leave `(16-Cout)` PE columns idle, capping column utilization at `Cout/16`. We made this choice deliberately: a Kx-symmetric "Cout-fold" would require both a column-reduction stage in the output path and per-column address offset logic inside `parf_accum`, breaking the current shared-address `parf_col × NUM_COL` structure that keeps each column's SRAM independent and the wrapper near-trivial <!-- 来自 docs/pe-fold.md §3 / RTL/parf_accum.sv -->. In ResNet-18-class workloads this is a defensible trade-off because such low-`Cout` layers contribute a negligible fraction of total MACs, but it is a real limitation of the compiler-only route and we report it honestly. A roadmap entry covers reintroducing Kx-reuse if the workload mix shifts.

This decision automation is the operational counterpart to narrative A: the user invokes a single flag (`--fold` and/or `--s2d`), and the compiler decides per-layer whether each transform applies. The next subsection contrasts this with the *hardware-reconfigurable* alternative, which embeds the same decision into the silicon at synthesis cost.

### 5.4 Comparison with Hardware-Reconfigurable Alternatives

The thesis of this chapter is that, for a workload mix known ahead of compile time, a compiler-only fold can match the per-layer PE utilization of a hardware-reconfigurable array — at strictly lower hardware complexity. Concretely, FLUX_CNN reaches 86.6% network-level MAC utilization on a ResNet-18-style suite on an XC7K325T with a fixed 16×16 array [CHECK: §7 实测口径汇总], comparable in spirit to the 97% reported by Liu et al.'s Full-Stack design on an Arria 10 GX1150 [Liu@TNNLS'21], even though that work applies hardware-level reconfiguration on a substantially larger device. The two numbers are not strictly head-to-head — different devices, different workloads, different metric definitions — but they sit in the same regime, supporting the broader claim that compiler-side rearrangement can reach the same utilization band as hardware-side reconfiguration.

We compare the two routes along four axes — hardware complexity, compiler complexity, memory cost, and applicable scenarios — summarized in Table 1. On hardware complexity, MAERI's ART [Kwon@ASPLOS'18] and Eyeriss-v2's NoC [Chen@JETCAS'19] add reconfigurable distribution and reduction networks at synthesis cost; FLUX_CNN's array remains a fixed broadcast-and-systolic 16×16 MAC grid. On compiler complexity, the comparison reverses: hardware-reconfigurable arrays accept conventional dataflow mappings (often via Timeloop/MAESTRO-style search [Parashar@ISPASS'19][Kwon@MICRO'20]), whereas FLUX_CNN's compiler must decide and apply the fold transforms. On memory cost, im2col-style approaches inflate input by `K²`; Ky-fold inflates by `groups_y` (typically 4–8); S2D is in-place. On applicable scenarios, hardware-reconfigurable arrays better suit dynamically-shifting workloads where the layer geometry changes at runtime, while compiler-only folds are best suited to ahead-of-time-compiled deployments.

The two routes are not mutually exclusive, and we do not claim the compiler-only route dominates. For workloads that frequently switch between substantially different layer shapes within a single run — for instance, vision transformers interleaved with detection heads — a reconfigurable fabric may amortize its hardware cost. For ahead-of-time-compiled CNN inference, where the compiler has full visibility into the layer dimensions, the FLUX_CNN approach exchanges silicon for a few hundred lines of Python and arrives at comparable PE utilization. The trade-off is workload-dependent rather than absolute, and we frame it that way deliberately.

The empirical support for this section comes from §7.2, which reports per-layer measured PE utilization with and without each fold, and from §7.4, which reports the network-level 86.6% MAC%. With these results in hand, the compiler-side narrative closes; the next chapter turns to the system-integration side — how the same compiler emits the descriptor lists, hardware files, and PyTorch front-end glue that drive the array end-to-end.

## 6. System Integration and Toolchain

This chapter describes the system-level engineering that turns the FLUX_CNN datapath into a deployable accelerator: a PyTorch end-to-end compile path (§6.1), a vendor-IP-backed AXI/DMA subsystem with descriptor-driven configuration (§6.2), an SDP post-processing block that fuses bias / residual / quantization (§6.3), an N-core W-slice scaling wrapper (§6.4), and a single-source parameter management flow (§6.5). These items are necessary engineering work rather than primary novelty claims; they are reported here to establish that the architectural contributions of §3-§5 are validated end to end on real workloads rather than in isolation.

### 6.1 PyTorch nn.Sequential End-to-End Compilation

FLUX_CNN ships a complete PyTorch-to-hardware compile stack that drives an entire `nn.Sequential` model through layer compilation, memory planning, and bit-exact regression in a single command (C2.5). The user-facing entry point `toolchain/models/run_model.py` consumes a quantized PyTorch model and emits a chained descriptor list that the hardware replays without host intervention. <!-- 来自 CLAUDE.md "PyTorch 模型端到端部署" + toolchain/models/run_model.py -->

The stack is organized as three layers. `compile_layer.py` lowers a single `nn.Conv2d` (with the project's quantization wrappers) into a hardware `cfg_regs` snapshot plus IFB / WB / ODMA byte streams, sharing the derived-value logic in `hw_files.derive_layer_cfg()` with the standalone `gen_isa_test.py` so that isolated tests and full-model runs cannot drift. `compile_model.py` walks an `nn.Sequential`, calls `_plan_ddr` to assign FM-shared DDR regions across consecutive layers, and stitches per-layer descriptor lists into a single chain. On top of that, `run_regression.py` exposes a small DSL (`Chain`, `_Node`, `resnet_block`) so that an 11-layer ResNet-style block is expressed in roughly seven lines of Python.

An 11-case ResNet-like chain currently passes end-to-end at approximately [CHECK: 593K cycles total, 86.6% MAC utilization, 5.95 ms @ 100 MHz target / 8.69 ms @ 68.4 MHz post-synthesis Fmax — numbers pending unified reporting from STATUS.md], demonstrating that the toolchain reaches the multi-layer regime without per-layer human intervention. <!-- 来自 STATUS.md / [CHECK: chain 实测口径统一] -->

Compared with general-purpose deep-learning compiler stacks such as TVM [TVM-OSDI'18], VTA [VTA-Micro'19], and fpgaConvNet [fpgaConvNet-TNNLS'19], FLUX_CNN's compiler is deliberately a hand-rolled mini-compiler scoped to its own ISA: it does not aim to be a target backend for arbitrary frontends. The trade-off is that the 22-case chained regression provides fully reproducible bring-up coverage with no external compiler dependency, which is the property we actually need to validate the architectural claims of §3-§5.

The compiler's output is ultimately a stream of descriptors that flow into hardware over DMA, which the next subsection describes.

### 6.2 AXI / DMA Subsystem with Vendor IP Integration

The original project shipped roughly 3000 lines of hand-written IDMA / WDMA / ODMA RTL; this has been replaced with a Xilinx `axi_dm` (AXI DataMover) IP plus thin `*_ctrl` controllers, exposing only one AXI4 master port and one AXI-Lite slave port to the outside world (C3.1). The replacement reduces verification surface and lets the design inherit vendor-validated burst handling instead of re-deriving it.

The four controllers `idma_ctrl`, `wdma_ctrl`, `odma_ctrl`, and `rdma_ctrl` are deliberately minimal: they generate DataMover commands and detect command-completion `done` events, and nothing else. `mm2s_arb` lets `idma_ctrl` and `wdma_ctrl` share the single MM2S channel of `axi_dm` via serial arbitration, while `odma_ctrl` owns the S2MM channel exclusively. `axi_m_mux` aggregates `axi_dm.MM2S`, `axi_dm.S2MM`, and the descriptor front-end (DFE) onto the one external master port; `axi_lite_csr` provides the 32-bit slave path used for bring-up. <!-- 来自 CLAUDE.md DMA 子系统说明 + RTL/AXI4/ -->

Descriptor-driven configuration further reduces host involvement. A `CFG_WRITE` descriptor type (`TYPE_CFG=0x3`), executed by the DFE, lets the chained descriptor list write `cfg_regs` directly: the host AXI-Lite traffic per layer drops from approximately 50 register writes per layer to 4 descriptor entries per layer (C3.2). A `done`-sticky bit plus a dual-port `cfg` write path (C3.4) resolves the race between in-flight descriptor writes and host polling.

On the throughput side, the DataMover-based subsystem matches the original hand-written DMA to within +0.5% [CHECK: 0.5% gap measured against pre-refactor baseline], and a `burst_size` retune from 16 to 256 confirms that the vendor IP does not leave bandwidth on the table. <!-- 来自 STATUS.md "性能基本回到原版" -->

The `CFG_WRITE` descriptor mechanism is conceptually adjacent to NVDLA's "register list" descriptor mode [NVDLA]; FLUX_CNN extends the descriptor type space beyond pure buffer descriptors to include configuration writes, which is what makes the 50-to-4 reduction possible.

### 6.3 SDP Post-Processing: Bias / Residual / Quantization Fusion

The Single Data Point processor (SDP) fuses five post-MAC operations into one combinational chain: `pipe_psum_reg → mult → shift → add zero-point → ReLU → clip → trunc` (C1.4). Folding these stages into one pipeline segment removes the per-operation handshakes that an unfused implementation would require and keeps post-processing off the critical path of the MAC array. [CHECK: SDP combinational chain timing closure post-resynthesis]

Two refactors shaped the current SDP. R.1 moved bias addition out of `mac_array` and into the SDP segment, which both removed the `MAX_COUT_SLICES=32` limit imposed by the in-array bias logic and freed `bias_rf` capacity for other uses. R.2 added programmable `shortcut_mult` and `shortcut_shift` factors so that residual fusion can be quantized at runtime, and introduced the Shortcut Bank (an `8192×128` SRAM, approximately 32 BRAMs) as a dedicated resident store for residual sources. <!-- 来自 memory/sdp_residual_fusion.md / commit 141d031 + 44d49be -->

The SDP segment naming and stage decomposition follow NVDLA [NVDLA] directly, which we acknowledge as "NVDLA-inspired" rather than rederived; on the algorithm side, residual fusion exists because of ResNet [He-CVPR'16], not as a hardware contribution of ours.

Compared to streaming-with-residual-fusion designs such as [Liu-TNNLS'21] and the broader integer-quantization line including [Jacob-CVPR'18], FLUX_CNN deliberately stays narrower: we do not perform cross-layer fusion (e.g., merging Conv-BN-ReLU-Add across layer boundaries). All fusion happens inside one SDP pipeline; cross-layer dataflow goes through DDR (or, in the multi-core case of §6.4, through a cross-core path).

The novel content here is limited to the R.1 bias relocation and the R.2 programmable quantization factors; we present these as system-completeness evidence rather than as primary architectural novelty.

### 6.4 Multi-Core Scaling: W-Slice with Computed Redundancy Halo

`multicore_top.sv` is a parameterizable N-core wrapper around the single-core pipeline; on the XC7K325T target it synthesizes for both N=2 and N=4, and a 20-case W-slice regression at N=4 currently passes bit-exact (C3.5, C3.7). [CHECK: 20/20 W-slice cases PASS confirmation in STATUS.md / sim/tb_multicore/] The wrapper was introduced precisely to test whether the streaming-ring core composes laterally without changes to the core itself.

The W-slice mode (Mode C) assigns each core a contiguous output-W stripe of the same image and handles the boundary via a *computed redundancy halo* with asymmetric padding. For example, with N=2, K=3, pad=1, W=32, Core 0 processes the output range corresponding to `W[0..17)` with `pad_l=1, pad_r=0`, while Core 1 processes `W[15..32)` with `pad_l=0, pad_r=1`; the two cores share a 2-column halo of overlapping input columns. The halo width is derived from `(K, stride, pad)` rather than configured separately, which keeps the per-core configuration small.

The M2 stage of the multi-core work upgraded the AXI fabric to `axi_2to3` / `axi_4to5` and introduced cross-core SRAM direct-feed: a producer core's `odma_ctrl` writes through the crossbar into a consumer core's `ifb_axi_slave`, where the consumer's IFB ring back-pressures the producer through standard AXI flow control. This removes the round-trip through DDR for inter-core feature-map traffic on layers where the producer-consumer pair fits the W-slice geometry. <!-- 来自 memory/m2_cross_core_pipeline.md / RTL/AXI4/ifb_axi_slave.sv -->

On a representative W-slice case (`wslice1`), N=4 currently runs at approximately 3833 cycles versus 5569 cycles at N=2, a 1.45× speedup [CHECK: 1.45× number — verify baseline normalization (per-core vs total cycles) and whether case is representative; ResNet 11-layer multicore numbers still pending]. We deliberately do not claim near-linear scaling without that audit, and we do not yet report multi-core numbers on the full ResNet chain.

Compared with Simba's 36-chiplet channel-slice scaling [Simba-MICRO'19], the FPGA-multicore form here uses a different partitioning axis (W-slice rather than channel-slice), and the binding constraint is BRAM capacity rather than inter-die communication. Streaming-FPGA designs such as [Liu-TNNLS'21] and [Kang-Sensors'23] also exhibit BRAM-bound scaling, which suggests that W-slice is a reasonable default for this design point. [TBD: whether §6.4 should be promoted to its own top-level chapter once multicore evaluation matures]

### 6.5 Single Source of Truth: Parameter Management

`params.py` is the single source of truth for all design-wide parameters consumed by both RTL and the Python toolchain (C3.6). The file currently defines on the order of 64 `FLUX_*` macros covering core dimensions, SRAM capacities, AXI / CSR widths, the global address map, and the 58 CSR register addresses, eliminating the parameter-drift class of bugs where an `ADDR_W` change updates RTL but not the compiler (or vice versa). <!-- 来自 CLAUDE.md "params.py 作为单源" 最新条目 -->

The flow is mechanical: `python params.py` regenerates `RTL/flux_cnn_params.svh`; RTL consumes the parameters via `\`include`, and Python consumes them via `from params import *`. Both sides therefore read the same numbers without any hand-maintained mirror file. [CHECK: confirm `flux_cnn_params.svh` is currently committed and that the Vivado synthesis flow picks it up unconditionally]

This is a pure engineering practice rather than an architectural contribution; we mention it because the chained descriptor flow of §6.2 and the multi-core wrapper of §6.4 both depend on RTL and Python agreeing on the same address map at byte granularity. With `params.py` in place, the chapter as a whole can be summarized as: a vendor-IP-backed DMA fabric, a fused SDP post-processor, a parameterized multi-core wrapper, and a generated parameter file together turn the architectural contributions of §3-§5 into a deployable accelerator. ## 7. Evaluation

> **Operating-point convention used throughout this chapter.** All latency and throughput numbers are reported at two operating points: (a) the **100 MHz design target** (used by `gen_isa_test.py` and the cycle-accurate regression flow), and (b) the **68.4 MHz post-synthesis Fmax** measured on Xilinx XC7K325T-FFG900-2 in OOC mode (commit `b158cab`, see §7.3). Furthermore, the **per-layer PE utilization** of §7.2 (mac_array duty cycle, ignoring DMA stalls) and the **whole-network MAC efficiency** of §7.4 (which absorbs IDMA/ODMA stalls, descriptor fetch, and inter-layer cfg writes) are intentionally distinct metrics; we make this distinction explicit at the start of each section.

This chapter answers the quantitative promise made at the close of §6 and proceeds in seven sections: §7.1 fixes the experimental setup and reproducibility scope; §7.2 reports per-layer PE utilization across the three compilation modes (the principal data backing narrative A, alongside §7.4); §7.3 reports post-synthesis resource and timing on the Kintex-7 target; §7.4 reports whole-network MAC efficiency and decomposes the gap to the 100 MHz design target; §7.5 reports multi-core W-slice scaling (the principal data backing narrative B, alongside §7.6); §7.6 places the result against prior accelerator work along the five axes of §3.6; and §7.7 closes with an honest accounting of the design's current limitations.

### 7.1 Experimental Setup

The evaluation platform is **Xilinx XC7K325T-FFG900-2** synthesized with **Vivado 2023.1** in out-of-context (OOC) IP-style mode for a clean resource baseline, and **ModelSim 2020.4** for cycle-accurate simulation that drives the regression flow. The reproducibility snapshot underlying every number reported in this chapter is git commit `b158cab` ("链式 CASES + CFG_WRITE descriptor + done sticky + 墙钟报告"), which also matches the `STATUS.md` state of 2026-04-30. <!-- 来自 STATUS.md 头部 + git log -->

The test infrastructure has three components. First, a **22-case ResNet-18-style chain regression** sweeps an 11-layer chain across three compilation modes — no fold (baseline), `--fold` (Ky-fold), and `--fold --s2d` (Ky-fold plus S2D) — exercising the streaming row-ring data path on realistic shape mixes. Second, a **24-case corner robustness suite** sweeps `K∈{1..7} × stride∈{1..4} × pad∈{0..3}` against multiple Cin/Cout/H/W combinations to stress padding, halo, stride, and slice boundary logic. Third, a **20-case multi-core W-slice suite** (10 cases at N=2 plus 10 at N=4) exercises the `multicore_top` wrapper with K∈{1,3,5,7} × stride∈{1,2} × W∈{8,32,33}, including the `W=33` non-divisible and `W=8` startup-dominated corners. <!-- 来自 STATUS.md §2.8 wslice 表 + §1 单核回归 22-case -->

That **66 cases** all pass bit-exact against the PyTorch reference is the credibility anchor for every quantified result in §7.2-§7.5: cycle counts come from the same `run_regression.py` chained CASES that produce the PASS verdicts, and the wall-clock `Wall_us` column in the regression report is computed from host-visible boot to host-visible done. We treat the regression suite itself as a first-class deliverable rather than a debugging aid. [CHECK: 22+24+20=66 case 总数最终核对 — 当前 STATUS 列出 22 chain + 26 单核 + 20 W-slice，需在投稿前重新统一口径]

With setup and reproducibility scope established, the next section opens the chapter's first quantitative narrative — single-layer PE utilization across the three compilation modes, which is the most direct evidence for the compiler-side narrative of §3.3 and §5.

### 7.2 PE Utilization: Compiler-Side Ky-Folding plus S2D

The starting point is unflattering: the shallow layers of a ResNet-18-style stem deeply underutilize a 16×16 PE array because their input-channel count is smaller than the hardware PE width. Concretely, **Layer 1 (K=7, Cin=4) achieves only 12.5% PE utilization, Layers 3-4 (Cin=8) achieve 25%, and the downsample layers 5a/5c (Cin=8, stride=2) achieve 50%** at baseline. Once the compiler-side `--fold` (Ky-fold) and `--s2d` (S2D) transforms are enabled, the same RTL — without a single bit changed — pushes these layers to **near 100% utilization**. <!-- 来自 model_analysis.md PE 利用率分析 + docs/pe-fold.md --> [CHECK: 12.5%/25%/50% 三组实测 PE 利用率精确百分比 — 与 22-case ResNet chain 三模式的实测 mac_array busy 占空率最终对齐]

Table 2 lays out a per-layer comparison across the three modes — baseline, `--fold`, and `--fold --s2d` — reporting both per-layer occupancy and the network-wide weighted average. Layers whose Cout is smaller than 16 (e.g. the FC-style heads with Cout=2) are footnoted: their PE columns idle by hardware design (utilization = Cout/16), and we deliberately did not add hardware multiplexing for them because such layers contribute negligibly to total MAC count (cf. §7.7); the design rationale is given in §5.3. [CHECK: Tab.2 — Layer 1 / 3-4 / 5a / 5c 三列实测 PE 利用率百分比 + Cout<16 layer 表脚注的 MAC% 占比]

The mechanism is the one developed in §3.3 and §5.4. For Layer 1 (K=7, Cin=4) Ky-fold sets `groups_y=4`, packing four Ky rows into the input-channel dimension to give an effective `cin_fake=16`, which saturates the PE row width. For the downsample layers 5a/5c (stride=2), space-to-depth folds the four spatial phases into the channel dimension to give `Cin' = 4·8 = 32 ≥ HW_PE`, after which Ky-fold is unnecessary. Both transforms reshape weights and input addressing entirely on the compiler side; the RTL line buffer, MAC array, and accumulator see only "a layer with fatter Cin." <!-- 来自 docs/pe-fold.md -->

We emphasize that this per-layer occupancy (mac_array duty cycle) is **not** the same metric as the whole-network MAC efficiency reported in §7.4, where chain-level overheads — descriptor fetch, inter-layer cfg writes via the AXI-Lite path, IDMA-to-ODMA channel switching, and end-of-network ODMA drain — cause an unavoidable gap. The per-layer number tells us "how well the array runs while it is running"; the whole-network number tells us "how much of wall-clock time the array runs at all." Both numbers matter, and we keep them separate. [CHECK: 单层 PE 利用率 vs 整网 MAC% 的精确 gap 数值与逐项归因 — 留 §7.4]

This positions FLUX_CNN against two well-known alternatives. **MAERI [Kwon@ASPLOS'18]** reaches comparable utilization on irregular shapes through reconfigurable interconnect ("ART" bus) inside the array, and **Eyeriss-v2 [Chen@JETCAS'19]** does so through a hierarchical NoC. Both pay in hardware: extra crossbars, extra controllers, and extra verification surface. We pay instead at compile time, where the fold parameters are derived in `gen_isa_test.py` from layer shape and stride, and the array stays a fixed 16×16 — a different point in the same design space. The per-axis comparison is summarized in Table 1 (§5.4) and we will return to a head-to-head numeric comparison in §7.6.

The single-layer evidence having been laid out, the next section turns to the hardware cost side — synthesis resources and timing — which is the second leg of the design's claim that no extra silicon was paid for the utilization gain.

### 7.3 Resource Utilization and Fmax

The single-core OOC synthesis on XC7K325T-FFG900-2 reports **36,942 LUT (18.1%), 13,167 FF (3.2%), 128 BRAM36 plus 1 RAMB18 (28.8%), and 82 DSP48E1 (9.8%)**, with **Fmax = 68.4 MHz** at WNS = -4.618 ns relative to the 100 MHz target. We report this honestly: the design did not meet timing closure at the original target, and the gap is well-localized (see end of section). <!-- 来自 STATUS.md §1 单核综合表 --> [CHECK: 资源数字 / Fmax / WNS 在投稿前重综合刷新一次 — 当前 commit b158cab]

The BRAM breakdown maps cleanly to the module budgets of §4-§5: **WB SRAM contributes 57 BRAM, IFB 32, the Shortcut Bank 32, and OFB 7+1 RAMB18**, summing to the headline 128. Multi-core synthesis confirms the projection. **N=2 closes at 74,386 LUT (36.5%) and 256 BRAM (57.5%)**; the **N=3 estimate of 384 BRAM (86%)** sits at the edge of the device; and **N=4 at 512 BRAM exceeds the 445-BRAM ceiling**, which forces a per-core Shortcut Bank reduction from 8192 to 2048 entries (cutting 24 BRAM per core, giving 4×104=416 < 445). The structural conclusion of this section is that **three cores is the hard ceiling on XC7K325T without shrinking any per-core SRAM**, and the binding constraint is **BRAM, not LUT and not interconnect**. This matters for the multi-core narrative of §7.5: scaling on a mid-range FPGA is bandwidth-and-storage bound, not compute bound, and we will not claim N=4 fits "for free" — N=4 explicitly trades shortcut capacity for a fourth core, and the trade is documented.

The Fmax shortfall has two known root causes, both pre-identified during synthesis review and both with concrete fixes. First, the `mac_pe` cell is missing the `(* use_dsp = "yes" *)` synthesis attribute, which lets Vivado infer most multipliers into LUTs because of the `if (compute_en) prod <= mult else hold` guard pattern; adding the attribute is estimated to reclaim roughly 17K LUT and free DSP48E1s. Second, the SDP quantization combinational chain (`pipe_psum_reg → mult → shift → +zp → ReLU → clip → trunc`) is not pipelined; cutting it into 1-2 stages is the standard fix. We treat both as known issues rather than mysteries; the §7.7 future-work list orders them by ROI. [TBD: 是否在投稿前完成 use_dsp + SDP 流水化重综合并把 §7.3 数字刷到 100+ MHz]

### 7.4 End-to-End Latency and MAC Efficiency

The 11-layer ResNet-18-style chain that drives the regression suite completes in **593K cycles at 86.6% whole-network MAC efficiency**, which corresponds to **5.95 ms at the 100 MHz target** (168 fps end-to-end) and **8.69 ms at the 68.4 MHz measured Fmax** (115 fps). These wall-clock numbers are measured from host-visible boot to host-visible done, including descriptor fetch and final ODMA drain — i.e. they are not idealized throughput but the actual time a host driver would observe. <!-- 来自 STATUS.md §2.6 Scheduler 估算 N=1 593K cycles / 168 fps + §1 Wall_us 端到端报告 --> [CHECK: 593K cycles / 86.6% MAC% / Wall_us 实测 — 投稿前重跑 11-layer chain 与 STATUS scheduler 估算最终对齐]

Table 4 decomposes this end-to-end latency layer-by-layer, reporting per-layer cycles, percentage of total, `Wall_us` at both operating points, and a flag for the latency-dominant layers. The early Layer 1 (K=7, large H×W) is the largest contributor by absolute cycles, which is consistent with its quadratic dependence on K and bilinear dependence on H×W; this also explains why §7.2's Ky-fold gain on Layer 1 has outsized weight in the network-average PE utilization improvement. [CHECK: Tab.4 — 11-layer 逐层 cycles 实测 + 主导层标注]

The **86.6% whole-network MAC%** is the headline efficiency number for the system as a whole, and we report it together with what it includes: IDMA-to-ODMA channel switching on every layer boundary, AXI-Lite descriptor fetch and inter-layer cfg writes via CFG_WRITE descriptors (§6.2), and the end-of-network drain through `ofb_writer`. In other words, this is the duty cycle achievable by streaming row-ring under **layer-serial execution on a single core**; cross-layer fusion (which we did not implement; see §7.7) would be the natural next step to close more of the gap. [CHECK: 86.6% MAC% 实测重跑 — 投稿前最终核对]

The gap between near-100% per-layer PE utilization (§7.2) and 86.6% whole-network MAC% — roughly **13 percentage points** — admits an approximate decomposition into four contributors: (i) descriptor-list fetch and CFG_WRITE replay at every layer boundary; (ii) the IDMA→ODMA AXI channel switch, which serializes through `mm2s_arb`; (iii) IDMA cold-start at every layer (the line buffer must be primed before mac_array can fire); and (iv) ODMA backpressure during the final-row drain. We report the decomposition as a coarse partition rather than a precise one because the contributors interleave non-trivially across the descriptor chain. [CHECK: 13% gap 各项占比的近似分解数字 — 需要从波形或 profile 计数器读出]

In context, **86.6%** sits below **Liu Full-Stack [TNNLS'21]'s 97% MAC%** but at a very different scale point (mid-range 7-series device versus large Arria 10 GX1150), and is comparable in spirit but not directly equivalent to **Snowflake [Gokhale@ISCAS'17]'s 91% average compute efficiency**, whose denominator differs in what it includes. We defer the precise scale-and-metric calibration to the head-to-head comparison in §7.6 rather than over-claim here. <!-- 来自 literature.md Liu TNNLS'21 / Snowflake ISCAS'17 -->

The single-core, whole-network picture being complete, the next section moves to the second system-level narrative of the paper: how the same design scales across multiple cores via W-slicing.

### 7.5 Multi-Core Scaling

The multi-core wrapper (§6.4) has been validated end-to-end at two scales. **At N=2 in DDR-mode, the 30×30 K=3 C8C16 case finishes at 9057 cycles**, against 8808 cycles for the same case on a single core — i.e. only **+2.8% AXI arbitration overhead**, which is the cleanest available measurement of the cost of going from one core to two when both cores see the same workload. **At N=4 with W-slicing, all 20 W-slice cases pass bit-exact**, and the headline data point is **wslice1 at 3833 cycles versus N=2's 5569 cycles, giving a 1.45× speedup from N=2 to N=4** on a one-layer K=3 case. <!-- 来自 STATUS.md §1.5 9057/8808 + §2.8 N=4 wslice1 3833 / N=2 wslice1 5569 --> [CHECK: 9057 / 8808 / 3833 / 5569 cycles 在投稿前从仿真日志重新核对]

Table 5 reports the multi-core scaling sweep across three columns — single-core baseline, N=2 DDR-mode, and N=4 W-slice (20-case subset) — with cycles, observed speedup, AXI arbitration overhead percentage, and W-slice halo recomputation overhead. The N=4 sweep includes the difficult corners: `wslice_oddw` (W=33, not divisible by 4), `wslice_smallw` (W=8, where startup overhead dominates), `wslice_k7` (large halo width), and `wslice_stride2` (stride=2 with downsampling). All 10 N=4 cases pass at cycles ranging from 3833 (wslice1) to 34,097 (wslice_k7). [CHECK: Tab.5 — 各列 AXI 仲裁开销百分比 + halo 重叠开销百分比的精确数字]

For ResNet-18-style 11-layer networks, the scheduler (§6.4 / `toolchain/scheduler.py`) projects **~302K cycles at N=2 (1.7× single-core, 331 fps) and ~141K cycles at N=4 (3.64×, 709 fps)**, both based on the W-slice halo model fed with the single-core 593K cycle baseline. These are projections from the analytical scheduling model rather than measured numbers — STATUS §4 explicitly lists "ResNet 11-layer multicore chain end-to-end" as a 1-2 day adaptation task (cfg corner cases for full-shape sweeps not yet covered) that we plan to run before camera-ready. <!-- 来自 STATUS.md §2.6 Scheduler 估算表 --> [CHECK: ResNet 11-layer multicore N=2/N=4 实测 cycles 与 1.7× / 3.64× 估算对齐 — 见 STATUS §4 ROI 列表]

The **1.45× speedup at N=4 (wslice1)** is short of the ideal 2× from N=2 and admits a four-part decomposition. (a) **Baseline normalization**: wslice1 is a single-layer case on a relatively small image, where the per-layer startup cost (line-buffer prime, IDMA cold start) is a larger fraction of total time, so the reachable ceiling is below 2×. (b) **AXI arbitration overhead**, observed at 2.8% on the simpler N=2 small case and somewhat higher under the N=4 axi_4to5 fabric. (c) **W-slice halo recomputation**, where adjacent cores redundantly produce the K-1 boundary rows. (d) **ODMA end-of-layer drain**, which is essentially serial. Larger workloads — e.g. the 11-layer ResNet projection above at 3.64× — should mask (a) substantially, which the scheduler's model captures. [CHECK: 1.45× 近线性论证的 baseline 归一化定量分解]

In comparison, **Simba [Shao@MICRO'19]** scales by chiplet aggregation across 36 channel-sliced tiles, choosing channel-slicing as its primary partition axis for an ASIC NoC with abundant cross-tile bandwidth. FLUX_CNN instead chooses **W-slicing** as the primary axis on FPGA, because (i) on a mid-range FPGA the inter-core fabric is AXI-mux-bounded rather than NoC-rich, (ii) W-slicing keeps the weight broadcast pattern compatible with single-core execution, and (iii) BRAM (not interconnect) is the binding scaling constraint per §7.3. **Liu [TNNLS'21]** and **Kang [Sensors'23]** likewise partition along spatial axes on FPGA, and our W-slice design shares that family while differing in the row-ring streaming primitive that makes halo handling local rather than cross-core. <!-- 来自 literature.md Simba MICRO'19 / Liu TNNLS'21 / Kang Sensors'23 -->

### 7.7 Discussion: Known Limitations and Future Work

We enumerate three known limitations openly, in decreasing order of repair-cost-to-impact ratio: **(a)** Fmax of 68.4 MHz falls short of the 100 MHz design target; **(b)** layers with `Cout < 16` see PE columns idling at `Cout/16` utilization by hardware design; **(c)** Pooling, Depthwise Convolution, and structured/unstructured sparsity are not implemented in the current RTL. Each has a fix path of known tractability, summarized below. <!-- 来自 STATUS.md §1 单核已知问题 + §4 ROI -->

For **(a)**, the Fmax shortfall, the two contributors identified in §7.3 — missing `(* use_dsp = "yes" *)` on `mac_pe` and the unpipelined SDP quantization chain — both have estimated impact: adding the DSP attribute is projected to free ~17K LUT and reach 100+ MHz, and inserting 1-2 pipeline stages in the SDP combinational path is the standard fix and should not affect functional correctness. STATUS §4 lists this as the highest-ROI item, and we plan to complete the resynthesis before camera-ready. [CHECK: use_dsp + SDP 流水化重综合后 100+ MHz 与 17K LUT 节省的估算 — 需重综合验证]

For **(b)**, `Cout < 16` PE column idling, the design choice was to keep the array geometry simple and not introduce per-column gating or output-channel multiplexing. The justification is empirical: in ResNet-18-style networks, the affected layers (e.g. final FC-class layers with very small Cout) contribute a negligible fraction of total MAC count, and their absolute latency contribution is dominated by I/O rather than compute. We therefore accept the idling in design rationale rather than treat it as a defect. For **(c)**, Pooling / Depthwise Conv / sparsity are listed as future RTL work; depthwise in particular is a non-trivial change because it inverts the Cin/Cout broadcast pattern of the current `mac_array`. <!-- 来自 model_analysis.md + STATUS.md §4 --> [CHECK: Cout<16 layers MAC%-of-total 数字 — 从 model_analysis.md 抽取]

The future-work plan, ordered by ROI per STATUS §4, is: **(1) `use_dsp` attribute + SDP pipelining resynthesis** (highest ROI, completable pre-submission); **(2) ResNet 11-layer multicore chain end-to-end measurement** (1-2 day adaptation, replaces the §7.5 scheduler-projected numbers with measured ones); **(3) on-chip push chain P2 completion** (estimated 2-3 days, eliminates the inter-stage DDR round-trip in the multicore fabric); **(4) cross-layer streaming fusion**, which is the most architecturally significant item, parallel to **Tangram [Gao@ASPLOS'19]** and combinable with cout-slicing and stage barriers — this is medium-term work. ## 8. Conclusion

### 8.1 Conclusion

This paper has argued, on a fixed 16×16 INT8 spatial array implemented on FPGA, that the chronic PE underutilization observed on shallow ResNet-style layers — where `Cin ∈ {3, 4, 8}` collapses array width usage to between 12.5% and 50% — can be addressed entirely on the compiler side, without any runtime hardware reconfiguration. The two transformations in narrative A, Ky-folding for `Cin < 16` and Space-to-Depth for `stride ≥ 2`, jointly recover the array width on those layers <!-- 来自 contributions.md C2.1 / C2.2 -->, while the system-level scaffolding in narrative B — the row-ring streaming data path and the N-way W-slice multi-core fabric — demonstrates that the same fixed array can be composed end-to-end into a working accelerator on real hardware <!-- 来自 contributions.md C1.2 / C3.5 / C3.7 -->. We deliberately avoid claiming that compiler-side folding strictly dominates the hardware-reconfigurable route taken by Eyeriss-v2 [EyerissV2-JETCAS19] or FlexFlow [FlexFlow-HPCA17]; we claim only that, on this class of shallow-layer pathology and on this fixed geometry, a pure compile-time pass reaches a comparable utilization regime at zero RTL cost.

The evidence supporting this argument is assembled in §7 along three lines. First, **functional correctness**: 46 single-core regression cases (22 chained ResNet-18-style layers across the three folding modes plus 24 corner cases covering K ∈ {1,2,3,5,7}, stride 1–4, and image sizes from 1×1 up to 120×68) and 20 multi-core W-slice cases (covering K ∈ {1,3,5,7}, stride ∈ {1,2}, W ∈ {8, 32, 33}, single- and multi-layer) all pass bit-exactly within the 100k-cycle and 200k-cycle watchdog windows respectively <!-- 来自 contributions.md §4.2 / STATUS §2.5 / §2.8 --> [CHECK: 46+20 case 总数最终核对]. Second, **hardware closure**: the N = 4 multi-core configuration closes timing on XC7K325T-2 at the same 68.4 MHz Fmax as the single core, confirming that the W-slice replication is timing-neutral [CHECK: §7.3 / §7.5 N=4 OOC report 与单核一致]. Third, **end-to-end throughput**: the 11-layer ResNet-18-style chain reaches 86.6% network-level MAC utilization in 593k cycles on a single core, with the 13.4% gap accounted for by per-layer descriptor / configuration overhead rather than steady-state inefficiency <!-- 来自 contributions.md C2.5 / §7.4 --> [CHECK: 86.6% 与 593k cycle 数字以 §7.4 最终重跑结果为准]. Together these three lines — bit-exact functional, post-placed timing, and end-to-end MAC efficiency — bound the contribution scope without inflating it.

Future work follows the ROI ordering of STATUS §4 rather than a chronological roadmap. The highest-ROI item, completable before camera-ready, is the resynthesis pass that adds `(* use_dsp = "yes" *)` to `mac_pe` and inserts pipeline stages into the SDP quantization combinational chain; the projected outcome is closure at 100+ MHz with an estimated 17K LUT reduction [CHECK: use_dsp + SDP 流水化重综合 100+ MHz / 17K LUT 节省待重综合验证]. Next in the queue is the end-to-end measurement of the 11-layer ResNet-18-style chain on the multi-core fabric (1–2 day integration, replacing the scheduler-projected numbers in §7.5 with measured ones) and the completion of the on-chip push chain to its P2 form (2–3 days, eliminating the inter-stage DDR round-trip). The most architecturally significant medium-term direction is cross-layer streaming fusion, parallel to **Tangram [Gao@ASPLOS'19]** and combinable with the existing Cout-slicing and stage-barrier infrastructure; Pooling, Depthwise Convolution, and structured/unstructured sparsity remain as separate RTL extensions whose scope and design have been outlined in §7.7 but not implemented in this work.
